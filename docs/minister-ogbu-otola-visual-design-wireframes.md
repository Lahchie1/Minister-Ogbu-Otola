# Minister Ogbu Otola Personal Website — Visual Design Direction + Wireframes

**Status:** Design direction defined. Ready for refinement or implementation.

---

## Design Direction

### Core Posture
Restrained. Serious. Content-first. No visual noise.

This is not a modern “inspirational church” site full of stock photos, gradients, and soft language. It is a minister’s teaching and resource site. The design must signal clarity, doctrinal seriousness, and trustworthiness.

### Visual Rules
- High readability over visual flair.
- Generous whitespace. Avoid cramped layouts.
- Limited color. One strong primary + one restrained accent is enough.
- Typography carries most of the hierarchy. Do not rely on heavy graphics.
- Mobile experience must be excellent (majority of Nigerian traffic will be mobile).

### Recommended Palette (Working)
- **Primary:** Deep navy / charcoal (`#1A2332` or similar)
- **Accent:** Muted gold or warm amber (used only for links, buttons, and key highlights)
- **Background:** Off-white (`#F8F7F4`) or very light warm grey
- **Text:** Near black for body, slightly softer for secondary text
- **Borders/dividers:** Very light grey
- High contrast required throughout.

### Typography
- **Headings:** Strong, slightly condensed sans or a clean serif with good presence.
- **Body:** Highly legible sans-serif (Inter, Source Sans 3, or system-ui stack).
- **Base size:** 17–18px on desktop, comfortable line height (1.6–1.7).
- Clear hierarchy: H1 dominant, H2 clearly secondary, body never competing.

### Imagery
- One professional photo of the minister on the About page (if available and appropriate).
- Book covers only on the Books page.
- No decorative stock photography, no random “worship hands” images, no heavy background patterns.

### Tone in UI Copy
Direct. Respectful. Free of marketing fluff.

### Accessibility Requirements
- WCAG AA contrast minimum.
- Proper heading hierarchy.
- Form labels and error messages associated correctly.
- Touch targets ≥ 44px.
- Keyboard navigable.

## Notes for Implementation
- Prioritise mobile readability and performance.
- Keep the design deliberately understated so the content (books, resources, teaching) remains the focus.
- Avoid any visual elements that could be interpreted as denominational branding or generic “Christian aesthetic.”
